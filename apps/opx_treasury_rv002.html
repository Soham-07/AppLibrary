Emergency Reserve

A pool of cash sized to your essential expenses, income stability and dependents — kept liquid, never invested for growth.

Base is 3 months. Add 3 for variable income, 1.5 for somewhat-variable, and 1 month per dependent (capped at a sensible ceiling). This is a starting heuristic, not a rule to follow blindly — a single income with a mortgage and three dependents needs more caution than the formula alone suggests.

Where to keep it

Emergency money's job is to be there when you need it, not to grow. Priority order: instant-access savings account → sweep-in FD / liquid fund → short-tenure FDs laddered so at least a slice matures every few months. Avoid equity, long-lock FDs, and anything that penalizes early withdrawal — the cost of illiquidity during an actual emergency is far higher than the yield you'd give up by staying liquid.

How Many Accounts Does a Person Actually Need

A general structure, not a rule — the point of separate accounts is behavioral friction between "spend," "save" and "owed to the taxman," not the number itself. Every extra account is also a recurring cost: minimum balance, KYC renewal, one more login to track.

Bank Directory — Who's Good For What

A general reference, not a ranking or endorsement. Coverage, fees and rates change — confirm current terms directly with the bank. Deposit insurance in India (via DICGC) covers up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor, per bank, across all accounts combined — worth knowing regardless of which bank you pick.
